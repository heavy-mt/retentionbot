## Findings and follow-up

The running module hash matched the server checkout. The high-rate DAG growth
started before redaction, so it is separate from the corrected historical
redaction branch behavior.

Inspection of the running Synapse 1.162.0 handler showed the per-room limiter
covering transaction lookup only. Its purge read lock and event creation call
were outside the limiter. The build patch moves that existing block inside the
limiter, preserving the send arguments and the purge read lock. It fails closed
for unrecognized handler layouts and is idempotent for the corrected layout.
The first run required a repeat after deploying the CI-tested image. Results
of that repeat are recorded below.

Serialization conflicts were also logged in `retention_invalidation_bump` and
`retention_cache_reset_queue_room`. Those operations eventually completed;
contention remains a separate optimization item.

After the high-rate test, `stream_ordering_to_exterm` had approximately 12.49
million live rows and total relation size 1697 MB, including earlier tests.
Without a per-room baseline its growth cannot be attributed precisely to this
run. Root free space decreased from approximately 7.46 to 5.63 GiB. No history
tables were manually deleted or vacuumed to hide the result.

After successful repeated load: start and manually test command-bot, then
complete installation and operations documentation.

- Server checkout: `10f5da3b3967c45352058f91a62732ba379f5c98`.
- Image: `retention-synapse:1.162.0-room-limiter-10f5da3`.
- Deployed at approximately 20:05 Moscow time; Synapse reported 1.162.0 and healthy.
- Running message handler SHA256:

- New room: `!FCvifAtMYlQahUzNwZ:matrix.heavyadmin.ru`.
- Same four requested stages, retention policy and workers. One systemd oneshot
  ran the stages sequentially; each stage observed for requested send duration
  plus 240 seconds from stage start. The terminal watch only read saved samples.

| Target msg/s | Requested send duration | HTTP accepted | Reported msg/s | Sampled max DAG tips | Final DAG tips |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 10 | 30 s | 300 | 9.99 | 1 | 1 |
| 25 | 30 s | 750 | 24.98 | 1 | 1 |
| 50 | 30 s | 1500 | 29.98 | 1 | 1 |
| 100 | 60 s | 6000 | 29.38 | 1 | 1 |

The first three stages passed the runner's completion checks: every tracked
message redacted, invalidations completed, jobs/dead queues empty, four workers,
and no original message events persisted. The 50 msg/s target was nevertheless
not achieved. These short bursts do not establish sustained retention capacity.

The 100 msg/s generator accepted all 6000 messages with zero HTTP errors.
HTTP latency p95 was 2588.16 ms; scheduling lag p95 was 133773.89 ms. The reported
rate implies approximately 204 seconds of actual generator elapsed time, rather
than the requested 60-second schedule. DAG tips stayed at one in every sample.

At t=304.8 seconds, the cumulative four-stage counts were 5202 redactions done,
127 missed (EVENT_PURGED), 3221 pending; invalidations 2737 done and 2592 pending.
The jobs queue had 4029 ready and four unacknowledged jobs. There were 5813
original message events and 2657 redaction events persisted. Deadlocks remained
31 (zero new); transaction rollbacks increased by 118 during that stage window.

The runner then stopped on incomplete retention/purge/queue checks. Its fixed
observation window was too short for the delayed end of message sending: the
last messages could become due for redaction around t=354 seconds and native
purge around t=384 seconds, plus the purge job interval. Future monitoring must
account for actual generator completion, rather than requested send duration.
This methodological error does not explain away the real EVENT_PURGED misses
or the failure to deliver the requested message rate.

A fresh observation at approximately 21:04 Moscow time confirmed recovery:
8423 redactions done, 127 missed, all 8550 invalidations done, both queues empty,
four consumers, DAG tips one, no original messages and one redaction persisted.
Thus the 100 msg/s stage ultimately redacted 5873/6000 messages; 127/6000 (2.12%)
were purged before confirmed redaction. Exact recovery time was not measured.
MemAvailable was 1832 MiB and root free space 6.08 GiB in that fresh sample.

Conclusion: sampled DAG growth is corrected under all four bursts. The 50 and
100 msg/s targets exceed the observed single-room admission rate in this setup;
100 msg/s also fails redaction coverage. This is not successful acceptance of
100 msg/s. Next investigate scheduling and processing latency before deciding
on further code or deployment changes.

Source inspection confirms redaction and invalidation share `retention.jobs`
and the same consumers, with prefetch one and no message priorities. FIFO
competition is a possible contributor, not yet an established cause of the
127 misses. A timestamped breakdown of event deadlines/completions and sampled
queue growth is needed before selecting a correction.
